Microsoft Live Share SDK licence (@microsoft/live-share 1.4.2, "SEE LICENSE IN LICENSE", research/meeting-app/_src/ls-package.json:6)

Use restriction, verbatim (_src/live-share-sdk-LICENSE.txt:12-13):

"a) General. You may install and use any number of copies of the software to develop and test your applications, solely for the purpose stated in 1(b) below and otherwise for your internal business purposes.
b) Purpose. The use of this license provided under these terms is specifically limited to developing applications that interact solely within the Microsoft Teams Ecosystem. You may distribute the software according to Section 3 of these terms."

Distribution (s.3): the code is distributable in your applications if you "adhere to the Purpose stated in Section 1(b)" and "indemnify, defend, and hold harmless Microsoft from any claims ... related to the distribution or use of your applications" (:22-24); you may not "use Microsoft's trademarks or trade dress in your application in any way that suggests your application comes from or is endorsed by Microsoft" (:26) nor put the code under a source-disclosure licence (:27). Scope (s.4): no stand-alone offering, no transfer (:32). Data protection (s.5): notices and consent for any data you collect through the software (:34). No warranty, damages capped at US $5 (:64-68). The repo README confirms the split: Live Share, media and canvas under this licence; Turbo/React under a pre-release licence; samples MIT (research/meeting-app/_src/ls-README.md:180-184).

What it means for us: (1) Using @microsoft/live-share with TestLiveShareHost + tinylicious inside the local Teams-twin is "develop and test your applications" for a Teams meeting app, inside s.1(a) - fine. (2) Using Live Share as a general transport for a non-Teams product (a public hopeOS page that is not a Teams app) would breach s.1(b). The twin must therefore stay a development harness for the Teams app, and the ws/WebRTC transports remain the non-Teams path. (3) Shipping the app to the pilot tenant is s.3 distribution: we owe Microsoft the indemnity and must not use Teams trade dress in the shipped app - which bears on how the twin is labelled if anyone outside the team sees it (section 4 below). (4) The FAQ adds: "Features in @microsoft/live-share ... don't work outside Microsoft Teams" (Live Share FAQ, retrieved 2026-09-18), so the technical scope matches the legal one. [counsel] on (2) and (3).

CDN-load vs self-host verdict. Today acs-client.html maps @azure/communication-calling to https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@azure/communication-calling@1.46.1/dist-esm/sdk.bundle.js (research/acs/acs-client.html:54-59). Two readings: (a) we do not "distribute" anything - each browser fetches Microsoft's published package from a public npm mirror, so s.2 is not engaged; (b) we "make available for third parties" object code through a host that is not Microsoft or an "authorized source" (s.9), with no integrity pin and with jsDelivr as an extra recipient of participants' IP addresses. Reading (b) is what a tenant security reviewer will apply. Recommendation: self-host dist-esm/sdk.bundle.js, LICENSE and NOTICES.txt unmodified under /vendor/acs-calling/1.46.1/ on our origin, served over HTTPS (the README requires https, :21), with a Subresource Integrity hash and a CSP that lists only our origin and *.communication.azure.com / *.skype.com endpoints the SDK needs. This is squarely s.2(a)(i) distribution, so the three s.2(b) duties attach: (i) our app adds the primary functionality - yes; (ii) terms.html must carry a pass-through clause binding end users to terms "that protect it and Microsoft at least as much" - drafted in terms.html section 4; (iii) we indemnify Microsoft - a hopeOS business decision. Loading from jsDelivr stays acceptable for engineering experiments on our own machines. [counsel]

4. Branding of the "Teams twin"

Microsoft Trademark and Brand Guidelines (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/intellectualproperty/trademarks/usage/general, retrieved 2026-09-18): third parties may "truthfully and accurately refer to Microsoft and its products and services" and "use without alteration in text" ("Contoso software works with Microsoft OneDrive"); "Our logos, app and product icons, illustrations, photographs, videos, and designs can never be used without an express license"; "Don't imply an affiliation, endorsement, sponsorship, or approval with or by Microsoft"; and a footnote such as "Microsoft, (list additional trademarks in alphabetical order) are trademarks of the Microsoft group of companies" is expected. Screenshots are governed by the "Use of Microsoft copyrighted content" page (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/legal/intellectualproperty/permissions, retrieved 2026-09-19): screenshots may be used in documentation, tutorials and websites if you "Do not alter the screenshot except to resize it", "Do not use portions of screenshots" and "Do not include screenshots in your product user interface", with the notice "Used with permission from Microsoft"; Microsoft's product icons "may not be used as a graphical or design element" and "cannot be modified or altered" (this covers Microsoft's proprietary product icons such as the Teams icon, not the MIT-licensed Fluent UI System Icons). The page does not address imitating a product's layout, so the twin's look falls back to trade-dress and the no-endorsement rules above. Both Microsoft licences we ship repeat the no-trade-dress-suggesting-endorsement rule (Live Share s.3(c)(i); ACS s.2(c)(i)), and the store guideline bans "Teams" in app names (1140.1.1).

Consequences: (1) Fluent tokens and icons are MIT code, so using them is licensed; the risk is trade dress - a page that *looks like* a Teams meeting. Inside the team it is a test harness and no issue. (2) If it is shown to the pilot partner or anyone else, it must carry a persistent, unmissable label ("hopeOS meeting sandbox - a local test harness that imitates the Microsoft Teams meeting layout for engineering. Not a Microsoft product; not affiliated with or endorsed by Microsoft." - consent-copy.md C10), must not show the Teams or Microsoft logo, the Teams wordmark in the chrome, or the purple "T" icon, and must not be named "Teams twin" anywhere a third party can see (internal nickname only; external name "hopeOS Meeting Sandbox"). (3) The manifests' names "hopeOS Ball Pass" and "hopeOS HoloHands" comply with 1140.1.1; the long name "hopeOS HoloHands video effect for Teams meetings" (research/video-effects/manifest.json:19) should read "... for Microsoft Teams meetings" on first reference (store guideline: "write the first reference ... as Microsoft Teams"). (4) Do not publish screenshots of the twin as if they were Teams. [counsel] on (2) if the sandbox is ever demoed publicly.
