# Positioning hopeOS as a Teams "add-on" (Teams vocabulary, verified 2026-09-18)

## 1. Teams' own app-type vocabulary and where hopeOS maps

Teams meeting apps are described by manifest capabilities and TeamsJS frame contexts, not by "AR overlay". The relevant ones, with the primary source for each:

| Teams term (manifest / SDK) | What it is | Does hopeOS fit? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meeting extension, side panel** (frame context `sidePanel`) | "allows users to pin your app as a tab in a meeting ... each user must open it individually"; "ideal for asynchronous scenarios during a meeting, such as searching for content to share to the meeting stage" | Yes, as the **controller**: pick the game, designate the goal tile, camera consent, latency HUD. Same role as Zoom's "Controller mode" sidebar app | [Live Share overview, meeting contexts](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/platform/apps-in-teams-meetings/teams-live-share-overview) |
| **Meeting extension, meeting stage** (frame context `meetingStage`, manifest `supportsCustomShareToStage`) | "allows users to share your app content to the meeting stage for participants in the meeting. In this context, users typically expect to collaborate in real time"; presenter-control patterns | Yes, as the **shared game surface** (ball, goal, scores), synced with Live Share. **The stage iframe cannot see or draw over the video tiles**; it sits beside the gallery | same; `supportsCustomShareToStage` in [meetingExtensionDefinition](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/extensibility/schema/root-meeting-extension-definition?view=m365-app-1.20) |
| **Live Share** (`@microsoft/live-share`; `LiveShareHost.create()`, `new LiveShareClient(host)`, `joinContainer(schema)`) | "a free, fully managed, and ready to use Azure Fluid Relay"; `LiveState` ("Synchronize any JSON serializable `state` value"), `LiveEvent` ("simple events ... only needed at the time of delivery"), `LivePresence`, `LiveTimer`, `LiveFollowMode` | Yes: ball **ownership** (`LiveState`), **throw/catch events** (`LiveEvent`), who is tracked (`LivePresence`), scores. | **Video effect / video filter app** (`meetingExtensionDefinition.videoFilters[]`, `videoFiltersConfigurationUrl`; TeamsJS `videoEffects.registerForVideoFrame`) | Your code processes each of **your own** outgoing frames; the result is what everyone else sees, no install needed on their side. | **Together-mode-like scene** (`meetingExtensionDefinition.scenes[]`, Scene Studio) | Static scene with seat rectangles; the client composited segmented participants | **Do not build on it.** "On June 30, 2026, Teams Together Mode will be retired. Custom Together Mode and Scene Studio in the Teams Developer Portal will no longer be available." | **ACS custom client** (`@azure/communication-calling` >= 1.13.1; `new LocalVideoStream(mediaStream)`, `setMediaStream`, `remoteVideoStream.getMediaStream()`) | Your own web page is the call client: own UI, own compositing, raw media both ways; Microsoft's own listed use case is "Augmented reality/Virtual reality: Remote incoming video media streams can be captured and augmented with a virtual environment before rendering on the screen" | Yes: the **only Microsoft path where our tracker can run on remote participants' frames** and where the ball can fly across real tiles. Caveats: raw media is **preview** ("without a service-level agreement"), and whether it works inside a *Teams meeting* via interop is undocumented (a Q&A answer says it does not). So in Teams' words, **hopeOS is a meeting extension (side panel + meeting stage, Live Share synced) bundled with a video-effect app**. The two halves cover the two things Teams lets a third party do: put shared UI on the stage, and change what your own tile shows. The cross-tile ball *inside real Teams tiles* is not achievable by a third party today (comparison.md, fact 1); it is achievable in an ACS custom client (if interop allows raw media) and in our own twin.

## 2. What "ours as an add-on with better usability and gameability" means concretely

The effect frameworks Teams and Zoom permit today are **per-user, self-only, cosmetic** (Maybelline looks, Zoom camera-mode overlays, Snap/Banuba lenses, Zoom Avatars). Games are **panel/stage apps with no relationship to the video** (Zoom Apps games, Meet add-ons, the retired Games for Work, Teams Carnival). The gap we fill:

1. **Effects that are interaction-ready, not cosmetic.** Objects placed by voice ("give me an apple in my hand") obey the prop-collision doctrine: hull-vs-hull, pickup only by finger wrap/clip/cradle, gravity always on, hands never pass through. No shipped video-call effect does physical hand-object contact; Modiface maps face points for makeup ([Maybelline post](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftteamsblog/announcing-the-new-maybelline-beauty-app-in-microsoft-teams/3877999)).
2. **Effects that cross tiles.** The Together-mode research finding, people "lean over and tap each other" across seats ([Microsoft Source, 2020-07-08](https://news.microsoft.com/source/features/work-life/microsoft-teams-together-mode/)), and Messenger's "Beach Bump" ("passing a beach ball back and forth", "up to six people at a time", 2018) ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2018/08/get-your-game-on-challenge-your-friends-with-video-chat-ar-games-in-messenger/)) are the two shipped precedents. One is retired, the other never reached enterprise. Our ball-pass with a designated goal tile is the enterprise version.
3. **Effects that are the same object for everyone.** Zoom camera-mode overlays are visible to all but private state; Meet co-doing and Live Share are shared state but no video. We combine: one authoritative game state (Live Share on Teams, our relay in the twin) and per-client rendering onto tracked hands.
4. **Camera-optional participation.** Zoom sells Avatars as usable "with your camera on or off" ([Zoom support](https://support.zoom.com/hc/en/article?id=zm_kb&sysparm_article=KB0059415)); a tracked body/hand layer gives people who keep cameras off, or who cannot use a mouse mid-call, a way to act in the meeting, in the browser, with no RTX requirement.

Positioning sentence for the pilot deck: *"hopeOS is a Teams meeting extension plus video-effect app. It ships the effect Teams does not have: tracked hands and bodies that can hold, pass, and throw shared objects across tiles, built to the same admin, consent, and store rules the Maybelline filter follows."*

## 3. What to borrow from Zoom Layers for the sandbox's own layer API

Zoom's Layers API is the only shipped, documented **API shape** for "an app becomes the meeting canvas". Copying its shape means a Zoom adapter later is thin. The pieces, with what we take:

| Zoom shape (verbatim names) | Why it is good | hopeOS twin equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Two app instances: a **controller** (`inMeeting`, sidebar) and a **rendered app** created by `runRenderingContext({view})` (`inImmersive` / `inCamera`); they talk with `postMessage`/`onMessage` ("Payload limit is <512KB"); `onRenderedAppOpened` signals readiness ([Using the Layers API](https://developers.zoom.us/docs/zoom-apps/guides/layers-using-api/), [ZoomSdk](https://appssdk.zoom.us/classes/ZoomSdk.ZoomSdk.html)) | Separates UI chrome from the render surface; the render surface can be a `<canvas>` while controls stay DOM | `layers.run({view:'immersive'|'camera'})` returns a `LayerContext`; controller <-> renderer via `BroadcastChannel` (same-origin) or `postMessage` (iframe) |
| `view: 'immersive'` (whole canvas, host-only, one at a time) vs `view: 'camera'` (self tile only, no host requirement stated) ([RunRenderingContextOptions](https://appssdk.zoom.us/types/ZoomSdkTypes.RunRenderingContextOptions.html), [Using the Layers API](https://developers.zoom.us/docs/zoom-apps/guides/layers-using-api/)) | Makes the permission boundary explicit: drawing on others is a host decision | Same two views; in the twin `immersive` needs the `host` role, `camera` does not |
| `drawParticipant({participantUUID, x, y, width, height, zIndex, cutout})`, `cutout` in `person | standard | rectangle | circle | square | verticalRectangle`; camera mode: self only ([DrawParticipantOptions](https://appssdk.zoom.us/types/ZoomSdkTypes.DrawParticipantOptions.html), [ParticipantCutoutShape](https://appssdk.zoom.us/types/ZoomSdkTypes.ParticipantCutoutShape.html), [Manipulating the UI](https://developers.zoom.us/docs/zoom-apps/guides/layers-manipulating-ui/)) | Participants are **layers with a z-order**, so a 3D object can be in front of or behind a person; `person` cutout = segmentation | `layers.drawParticipant({id, x, y, width, height, zIndex, cutout:'person'})`; our segmentation from MediaPipe Image Segmenter, our hands from holohands |
| `drawImage({imageData, x, y, zIndex})` takes a real `ImageData`; `drawWebView({webviewId, x, y, width, height, zIndex})`; "There is no in-place update: always clear + redraw" (`clearParticipant`, `clearImage`, `clearWebView`) ([zoom/skills layers-api.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zoom/skills/main/skills/zoom-apps-sdk/references/layers-api.md), [Manipulating the UI](https://developers.zoom.us/docs/zoom-apps/guides/layers-manipulating-ui/)) | Retained-mode layer list, deterministic composition | `layers.drawObject({objectId, mesh, zIndex})` plus `layers.clear*`; keep the clear-then-redraw rule so a Zoom adapter is a pass-through |
| Coordinates: immersive = CSS px of the app window; camera = "raw pixels, relative to the renderTarget dimensions returned by config()" (default 1280x720) | Forces the app to size to the target, not the screen | `layers.renderTarget` = tile pixel size in the twin; ACS/Zoom adapters map to theirs |
| `getMeetingParticipants()` ("Supported roles: Host, Co-Host"), `sendAppInvitationToAllParticipants()`, `startCollaborate/joinCollaborate` | Roster and invite are separate from drawing | `layers.participants()` + `layers.inviteAll()`; roster from the twin's room server, or Live Share `LivePresence` on Teams |
| What Zoom does **not** give: frames (`Apis` union has no frame method) ([Apis](https://appssdk.zoom.us/types/ZoomSdkTypes.Apis.html)) | Marks exactly where our twin exceeds Zoom | `layers.frames(participantId)` returns a `MediaStreamTrack` in the twin and ACS adapters; throws `NotSupportedError` in Zoom/Teams adapters |

Two shapes from other vendors worth folding in:

- **Teams `videoEffects`' dual handler.** The host "may support either VideoFrameHandler or VideoBufferHandler, but not both", so the app supplies both ([videoEffects](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/javascript/api/@microsoft/teams-js/videoeffects?view=msteams-client-js-latest)). Our `camera` view should expose the same pair so the Teams adapter is direct.
- **Zoom Video SDK's worker-hosted `VideoProcessor`** (`processFrame(VideoFrame -> OffscreenCanvas)`, created with `stream.createProcessor`, attached with `stream.addProcessor`) ([Raw data, video](https://developers.zoom.us/docs/video-sdk/web/raw-data-video/)). Running tracking + compositing in a worker is how we meet Teams' 22 fps floor; the twin's `camera` view should be worker-first.

See `examples/hopeos-layers-api.js` for the proposed surface and `examples/zoom-layers-app.js` for the Zoom skeleton it mirrors.

## 4. Gating summary for the pilot conversation

- **Teams video-effect path:** Beta API; manifest properties only in the `m365-app-prev` moniker (page dated 2026-08-11); explicit user consent; tenant admin toggle; desktop-only and EDU-off at the 2023 launch. Ask Microsoft whether third-party `videoFilters` are still accepted for new store submissions in the new Teams client and on which platforms ([videoEffects](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/javascript/api/@microsoft/teams-js/videoeffects?view=msteams-client-js-latest), [manifest](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/extensibility/schema/root-meeting-extension-definition?view=m365-app-1.20), [Video filters post](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/microsoftteamsblog/introducing-video-filters-in-microsoft-teams-meetings/3764187)).
- **Teams stage + Live Share:** GA, free relay, needs sideloading or store; Live Share has its own license ([Live Share overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/platform/apps-in-teams-meetings/teams-live-share-overview)).
- **Together mode:** retired 2026-06-30; do not mention it as a target ([What's new](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoftteams/platform/whats-new#deprecated)).
- **ACS raw media:** preview, no SLA; Teams-meeting interop undocumented ([RAW media overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/communication-services/concepts/voice-video-calling/media-access)).
- **Zoom (if asked "why not Zoom first?"):** Layers immersive is host-only, no frame access, beta apps limited to the developer account until "Request to Share" review ([Sharing private and beta apps](https://developers.zoom.us/docs/distribute/sharing-private-and-beta-apps/)). Same ceiling as Teams for cross-tile tracking; Zoom Video SDK (own client, per-minute) is the Zoom analogue of ACS.
- **Google Meet:** add-ons GA (2024-09-11) but no media at all; Co-Doing "closed to new signups"; the Meet Media API gives remote streams but is Developer Preview and needs every participant enrolled ([Co-Doing guide](https://developers.google.com/meet/add-ons/guides/use-CoDoingAPI), [Meet Media API](https://developers.google.com/workspace/meet/media-api/guides/overview)).
